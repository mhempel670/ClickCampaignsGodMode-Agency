What Makes This Different From the Assessment

The Assessment was intentionally expansive. It helped you see the accumulated value of your experience, recognize patterns, and consider credible next-chapter possibilities. The Blueprint is intentionally narrower and more concrete.

Here, you and Ben will look closely at the directions that resonated, challenge a few assumptions, identify what is realistic for your life, and choose where to place your attention now. The goal is not another heroic list of 47 things you could do. It is to decide what is worth doing next—and how to learn by doing it.

This is where reflection becomes direction, and direction becomes a plan that fits your actual life. A novel concept, perhaps, but a useful one.

What Your Written 90-Day Blueprint Will Contain

  1. Your chosen direction and 90-day outcome
    A concise statement of what you are pursuing now, why it fits, and what progress will mean.
  2. Three focused 30-day phases
    A practical sequence that moves from exploration and setup into real-world testing and informed next decisions.
  3. Weekly actions and commitments
    Specific, manageable steps that turn the plan into movement without creating another full-time job.
  4. Low-risk experiments
    For each test: what to try, why you are trying it, what evidence to watch, and what decision the evidence will inform.
  5. Risks, boundaries, and safeguards
    What could pull the plan off course, what you do not want to recreate, and the limits that protect your time and energy.
  6. Progress measures and decision points
    Simple signals that help you know whether to continue, adjust, pause, or choose another direction.

How to Get the Most From This Conversation

  • Correct anything from the Assessment that no longer feels accurate.
  • Say what genuinely excites you—and what leaves you cold.
  • Be candid about time, money, health, family, technology, and other real constraints.
  • Ask Ben to slow down, go deeper, compare options, or explain his reasoning.
  • Prefer useful evidence and small tests over pressure to make one permanent decision.

You remain the decision-maker. Ben helps you think, organize, question assumptions, and plan. Your experience and judgment decide what belongs. Ben is helpful; he is not the boss of you.
