# Graybeard Blueprint Strategy Session

## Matt's Conversation Guide

**Status:** Working draft for Matt's review  
**Length:** Approximately 60 minutes  
**Format:** Live, one-to-one conversation  
**Starting point:** The participant's completed Graybeard Assessment and Master Document

---

## What This Call Is For

The Blueprint Strategy Session gives the participant an hour with Matt to look at the possibilities uncovered in the Graybeard Assessment and talk through what might come next.

The aim is to help the participant get clearer. That may mean narrowing a long list to a few realistic possibilities. It may mean identifying one direction worth trying. It may mean discovering that an idea which looked good on paper does not fit at all. It may simply mean having a useful conversation that brings something new into view.

There is no requirement to choose a business, make a firm commitment, or leave with life figured out. Paid consulting, fractional work, employment, teaching, mentoring, volunteering, nonprofit or board service, a small business, a simpler role, and blended paths all count. Continued exploration also counts.

The call belongs to the participant. This guide gives Matt a road map, not a script he has to march through.

---

## The Tone

Think two people sitting at a bar or across a kitchen table, talking about something that matters.

- Plain English
- Peer to peer
- Curious without interrogating
- Practical without rushing
- Serious subject, light touch
- A little dry humor when it fits
- No guru act, therapy language, or motivational speech

### Use language like this

- "What gets you going in the morning?"
- "Which of these ideas keeps pulling you back?"
- "What could you see yourself doing without hating every minute of it?"
- "Which one sounds good on paper but probably isn't you?"
- "Are you looking to make some money, stay useful, help somebody, keep busy, or some combination of the above?"
- "If nobody cared what title you had, what would you actually want to do?"
- "What do you absolutely not want the next chapter to look like?"
- "Do you want to do something with this now, kick the tires for a while, or leave it on the shelf and think?"

### Avoid language like this

- "What feels alive for you?"
- "Where are you experiencing resonance?"
- "What is your authentic contribution?"
- "What does your emotional truth tell you?"
- "What are you willing to commit to today?"

## Before the Call

### Matt reviews

- The participant's Master Document
- The strongest areas of experience and hard-won knowledge
- The range of paths identified in the Assessment
- The participant's stated preferences, constraints, and things to avoid
- Any questions, contradictions, or surprising possibilities worth discussing
- Anything the participant specifically asked to cover

### Matt prepares

Write down no more than five items:

1. Two or three possibilities that appear especially worth discussing
2. One apparent contradiction or tradeoff
3. One thing the participant may be overlooking
4. One practical question that could separate an interesting idea from a workable one
5. One reminder not to assume the participant wants a business or paid work

### Optional participant preparation

Ask the participant to review the Master Document and mark:

- What sounded right
- What did not sound like them
- What surprised them
- What they keep thinking about
- What they want to make sure gets discussed

No homework is required. The call should still work if they arrive with nothing marked and say, "All right, where do we start?"

## A Loose 60-Minute Road Map

The time ranges are guides, not rules. If a useful conversation takes an unexpected turn, follow it.

### 1. Settle In and Find Out What Is on Their Mind — 5 to 10 minutes

The purpose is to start like human beings, not launch into a document review.

Possible openings:

- "How are you doing? What's been going on since you finished the Assessment?"
- "You had a chance to look through the Master Document. What jumped out at you?"
- "Anything in there make you say, 'That's exactly right'—or 'Where the hell did that come from?'"
- "Before we get into my questions, what do you want to make sure we talk about today?"

If the participant wants to spend time talking about work, family, health, grandkids, or what has been rattling around in their head, let the conversation breathe. That context may explain what they want next better than another checklist would.

### 2. Sort the Master Document — 10 to 15 minutes

Separate what fits from what does not.

Questions Matt can use:

- "What did we get right?"
- "What did we miss?"
- "Which possibilities caught your attention?"
- "Which ones can we cross off without wasting another minute on them?"
- "Was there anything you had not considered before?"
- "What do other people keep coming to you for?"
- "What have you done for so long that you barely count it as a skill anymore?"

The goal is not to defend the Master Document. It is working material. Keep what helps and discard what does not.

### 3. Kick Around the Possibilities — 15 to 20 minutes

Explore the options that remain without treating every interesting idea as a new career.

Questions Matt can pull from:

- "Which of these would you actually look forward to doing?"
- "What kind of people do you want to spend your time around?"
- "How much responsibility do you want at this point?"
- "Do you want to lead something, advise from the side, teach, make, serve, or just help where you can?"
- "How important is income? Is it necessary, nice to have, or beside the point?"
- "How much time do you honestly want to give this each week?"
- "What pace sounds good now?"
- "Would you rather work with one person, a small team, a classroom, a board, or a whole community?"
- "Do you want to build something of your own, plug into something that already exists, or mix the two?"
- "What would make a perfectly good opportunity a bad fit for you?"
- "If you tried this and it went well, what would 'well' look like?"

Keep all paths legitimate:

- Consulting or advisory work
- Fractional or part-time leadership
- Employment in a different role or at a different pace
- Teaching, training, writing, or knowledge transfer
- Mentoring or coaching
- Volunteer, civic, faith-community, or community service
- Nonprofit, foundation, association, or board service
- A small business or independent practice
- A blended portfolio of paid work, service, and personal projects
- Continued exploration before selecting anything

### 4. See Where They Are — 10 to 15 minutes

Do not force a conclusion. Help the participant name where things stand now.

Possible questions:

- "Are we any clearer than we were an hour ago?"
- "What is still in the running?"
- "Did anything move up or down the list?"
- "Is there one option you want to look at first, or are we still comparing a few?"
- "What would you need to know before you could decide whether this is worth pursuing?"
- "Would it help to test something small instead of trying to decide the whole thing in your head?"
- "What, if anything, would be useful to do next?"

The conversation may land in any of four useful places:

1. **Exploring:** Several possibilities remain open.
2. **Narrowing:** A short list has emerged.
3. **Testing:** One or more options are ready for small, low-risk experiments.
4. **Moving:** The participant has selected a direction and wants an action plan.

None of these is a lesser result. The Blueprint should reflect the participant's actual position rather than inventing certainty.

### 5. Wrap Up Without Turning It Into a Sales Call — 5 minutes

Matt briefly plays back what he heard:

- What appears to interest the participant
- What appears to be off the table
- What questions remain
- Any next step the participant chose
- What Matt will use to prepare the 90-Day Blueprint

Possible closing language:

> "Let me make sure I've got this right. We have not carved anything into stone, but it sounds like these are the two ideas worth keeping on the table, this one probably needs to go, and the next useful move is to learn a little more about ____. Is that a fair summary?"

If the participant is ready to move:

> "It sounds like you have a direction you want to try. Good. We do not need to plan the next ten years today. Let's put together a sensible 90 days and see what the real world tells us."

If the participant is not ready:

> "You do not have to manufacture an answer because the clock is running out. We have narrowed the field and identified what you need to learn next. That's useful."

## Possible Outputs From the Call

Depending on where the participant lands, Matt may have enough to produce:

- A clearer summary of what fits and what does not
- A short list of directions worth further consideration
- Questions the participant needs to answer before deciding
- A set of small experiments or conversations
- One direction to explore first
- A practical 90-day action plan
- A blended plan combining paid work, service, learning, and personal priorities

The output should never claim the participant selected a path they did not select.

## The Graybeard Forum Invitation

Near the end of the call, Matt can invite the participant to join the Graybeard Forum.

The Forum is not presented as a cure, a mandatory next step, or a room full of people following the same business formula. It is a place for seasoned professionals who are all working through some version of the same broad question: what comes next, and what are they going to do about it?

Suggested language:

> "One other thing I'd encourage you to do is join us in the Graybeard Forum. Everybody there is heading in a different direction, but they're all working through the same general next-chapter question. Some are looking at paid work. Some want to teach, mentor, volunteer, serve on a board, or build something. Some are still figuring it out. It gives you people to compare notes with, and once you decide to put something into motion, it gives you a place to keep moving."

If a coaching cohort is available, Matt may explain that it adds structured implementation support. The invitation should stay separate from the participant's session outcome. A person does not need to buy coaching for the session to have been worthwhile.

## Notes for the 90-Day Blueprint

Immediately after the call, Matt should capture:

- What the participant responded to most strongly
- What they rejected or ruled out
- Their current stage: exploring, narrowing, testing, or moving
- The paths still under consideration
- Important constraints involving time, income, health, family, pace, responsibility, or technology
- Any action or experiment they chose
- Statements that should be preserved in the participant's own words
- Questions still unanswered
- Anything the AI must not infer

The separate Blueprint-generation process will use these notes, the participant's Master Document, and any transcript the participant has consented to use. AI prepares a draft. Matt reviews, corrects, and approves it before the participant receives it.

---

## Working Guardrails

- The participant's hour belongs to the participant.
- The road map serves the conversation; the conversation does not serve the road map.
- Do not assume that income or business ownership is the goal.
- Do not push for a decision because the hour is ending.
- Do not confuse an interesting possibility with a workable fit.
- Do not manufacture certainty in the follow-up document.
- Do not turn the final five minutes into a coaching-program pitch.
- Keep private participant materials private unless the participant explicitly agrees otherwise.
- Recording, transcription, AI use, storage, access, and retention rules require a separate approved consent policy.
