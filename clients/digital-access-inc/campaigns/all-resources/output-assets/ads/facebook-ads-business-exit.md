If you're thinking about selling your business in the next couple of years, I want to share something most business brokers won't tell you — because they get paid when you sell, not when you maximize what you sell for.

The gap between a business that sells at 3x EBITDA and one that sells at 5.5x is almost never about revenue.

It's about whether the buyer's due diligence team finds problems — or whether you found them first and closed them.

Here's the math on why that matters:

A home services business with $400,000 in EBITDA.

At 4x: $1.6 million exit.
At 5.5x: $2.2 million exit.

Same business. Same revenue. Same team. $600,000 difference.

---

What creates that gap?

In almost every local service business a buyer looks at, they find the same five things:

**Owner-dependent revenue.** If the business runs because you're in the middle of everything — your relationships, your decisions, your presence — buyers discount that. Because when you leave, they're not sure the revenue follows.

**No documented systems.** You know how your business works. But if I asked you to hand me the document that explains how you qualify a lead, handle a problem customer, or onboard a new technician — most owners say, "That's just how we do it." To a buyer, that's not a business. That's a job.

**Marketing gaps that signal risk.** Low review count. Page 2 rankings. No presence on AI search platforms. Every one of these is a question a buyer asks about future revenue stability. Closing these gaps before due diligence changes the conversation entirely.

**Inconsistent revenue.** Seasonal swings tell buyers you have a spike, not a system. A documented marketing engine that generates consistent monthly lead flow tells a completely different story.

**No performance track record.** One strong year is a snapshot. Eighteen months of upward-trending, attributable revenue growth is a trend line. Buyers apply multiples to trend lines.
