## GAP #1: OWNER-DEPENDENT REVENUE

**[SLIDE CHANGE: Image representing a business tied to one person]**

Picture this:

You're a roofing contractor. You've been in business 22 years. You know every major general contractor in your market. You know every property manager. You built those relationships over two decades.

When a buyer looks at your business, the first question they ask is:

*"Does this revenue come with the business — or does it come with him?"*

**[PAUSE - 2 seconds]**

If the answer is "mostly him" — they discount the price. Heavily.

It's called key-man risk. And it's one of the first things sophisticated buyers screen for.

**[PAUSE]**

Here's what the data shows:

Businesses where revenue is demonstrably tied to the owner's personal relationships, personal presence, and personal decision-making typically sell at the **lower end of the multiple range** for their industry.

Businesses where revenue is generated by a documented, systematic, AI-driven marketing engine — one that generates leads, answers calls, follows up, and books jobs **without the owner in the middle of every transaction** — command meaningfully higher multiples.

**[PAUSE - 2 seconds]**

The Maven analysis shows you exactly how owner-dependent **your** revenue currently is — and builds a roadmap to systematize the marketing and lead generation functions that buyers want to see running on their own.

When a buyer looks at **your** business and sees: *"This generates revenue even when the owner is on vacation"* — that's worth something significant at the table.

## GAP #2: INCONSISTENT OR SEASONAL REVENUE

**[SLIDE CHANGE]**

Buyers want to underwrite predictable earnings.

If your revenue is lumpy — big months followed by slow months, heavy seasonal swings, or a few large clients that represent most of the income — they have two options:

They discount the price. Or they walk away.

**[PAUSE]**

Here's a real example of what this looks like in the numbers.

Imagine a roofing company doing $2.1 million in revenue. But $800,000 of that comes in a 90-day window every spring. The other three quarters are thin.

A buyer modeling that business doesn't see $2.1 million in consistent revenue.

They see a seasonal spike they're not sure they can replicate — and they price accordingly.

**[PAUSE - 2 seconds]**

Now imagine the same business — same total revenue — but with a documented marketing system that generates consistent inbound leads every month of the year.

SEO traffic that doesn't stop in November. Review-driven "near me" searches that keep the phone ringing in January. An AI voice agent that captures every inquiry regardless of season.

Suddenly that buyer sees something completely different.

**[PAUSE]**

Not a seasonal business.

A consistent business that happens to peak in spring.

**[PAUSE - 2 seconds]**

The Maven analysis shows you what **your** revenue consistency looks like to a buyer — and what a systematic marketing engine that generates predictable monthly lead flow would mean for **your** EBITDA picture.
